# Larger First Encounter language — retained, not in this build

This document preserves the direction of the conversation so the narrow hands milestone does not erase it. It is not an instruction to implement the following in the current run. Latest owner corrections outrank older source notes.

## Physical continuity

The eventual ground game is hand-led, the air game kick/momentum-led. Jumping, rising knees, descending attacks, flying/bicycle kicks, wall boosts, vaulting, hanging and release should form continuous motion rather than separate minigames. Grab is physical attachment, not merely grabbing an enemy. Ground footwork is free within valid surfaces; authored elevated rails/nodes guide the larger traversal. Throws arise from bounded movement and release, not unlimited mouse spinning.

The world is a stunt set: authored intact/damaged/broken states change support, passage and cover. It is not a demand for granular fracture. Vehicles and hazards provide dangerous timing and opportunities. Both combatants can exploit or suffer those consequences. Environmental positioning should explain the outcome afterward even when the exact sequence is surprising.

## Clinch and finishers

The later clinch was discussed as a short contest after earning an opening. The written clinch note says attacker needs one win; defender can escape after two or risk continuing for a third to reverse. The exact choice cycle was not settled and subsequent conversation explored punch/kick/guard. Do not freeze an old push/pull/twist scheme or infer new rules from an anecdote. Tells, ties, failure, interruption and opportunity costs require a later test.

Latest finisher direction: combatant finishers should favor existing poses/physical consequences plus first-person hand/weapon actions, not a bespoke paired full-body cinematic for every kill. Elaborate damsel systems are out of this game's current slice altogether.

## Punchlines, BASED and KeyWrapping

A meaningful event can become material for a quick response: a gesture, bow, brief line, threat, challenge, or offer. That material should reference an actual event and its legitimate observers; the animation cannot create semantic truth. BASED's B/A/S/E/D ordered pairs shape delivery. A character's own profile, circumstances and observed history affect interpretation. The player is not exempt merely because its controller is human. Exact hidden weaknesses and stat mappings remain experimental.

The discussed wager: the challenged fighter may briefly gain force but lose speed, creating a dangerous response opportunity. A challenge genuinely left unanswered may cost accumulated Momentum/multiplier; answering need not mean rushing blindly forward. A counter, interception or clever environmental reply may count when properly defined. The same rules must apply both ways. Timers, magnitudes, repeat penalties and response eligibility are not settled. This is not implemented by the current optional Momentum gain experiment.

Style/prestige may later reward the risk of answering and performing, separate from winning the bout. No progression currency, leaderboard or multiplayer is required now. The opponent can show off and taunt, but the first partner need only make the basic exchange legible.

An offered hand can be sincere, patronizing, or bait. Acceptance and subsequent betrayal are different events. Private intent is not automatically known to the recipient, and physical animation does not determine social interpretation. This is the Encounter connection to preserve, not a reason to import a complete dialogue simulation into each contact tick.

## The larger ambitions

First Encounter is an immediate action expression of the shared-world philosophy. Trapstar remains a possible later product, not a promise or dependency. A useful first exchange tests a foundation; it does not certify a city-scale simulator. The creative ambition is large. The acceptance gate remains small and playable.
